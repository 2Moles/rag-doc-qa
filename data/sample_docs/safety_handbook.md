# Northfield Mining Co. Underground Safety Handbook (fictional sample)

This handbook is a fictional document written to demonstrate the rag-doc-qa project. Northfield Mining Co. does not exist.

## Gas monitoring

Every underground crew carries at least one calibrated multi-gas detector. Detectors measure methane (CH4), carbon monoxide (CO), hydrogen sulphide (H2S) and oxygen (O2).

Methane is measured as a percentage of the lower explosive limit (LEL). At 1.0% methane by volume, the section supervisor must stop cutting and increase ventilation. At 1.5% methane, all electrical power to the section is cut and non-essential workers withdraw to fresh air. At 2.0% methane, the entire section is evacuated and only the mine rescue team may re-enter.

The carbon monoxide alarm is set at 25 ppm, which is the eight-hour exposure limit. Any reading above 50 ppm requires immediate withdrawal and a report to the control room within 10 minutes.

Oxygen below 19.5% is treated as an oxygen-deficient atmosphere. Workers must not enter an area with less than 19.5% oxygen without self-contained breathing apparatus.

## Detector calibration

Gas detectors are bump-tested at the start of every shift. A full calibration with certified test gas is done every 30 days, or immediately after a detector is dropped or gets wet. A detector that fails a bump test is tagged out and returned to the lamp room; it must not be used until it passes a full calibration.

Calibration records are kept for three years and are reviewed by the safety officer every quarter.

## Ventilation

Main fans run continuously. If a main fan stops for more than 15 minutes, all workers leave the underground workings until airflow is restored and the gas readings are confirmed normal by a competent person.

The minimum air velocity at a working face is 0.3 metres per second.

## Emergency procedures

Every worker carries a self-rescuer rated for at least 60 minutes. Refuge chambers are located no more than 750 metres apart along the main haulage roads.

On hearing the evacuation alarm, workers stop work, switch off equipment, and walk to the nearest refuge chamber or the shaft, whichever is closer and upwind of the incident. The shift boss takes a head count at the assembly point and reports it to the control room.

Mine rescue drills are held every six months for every crew.
